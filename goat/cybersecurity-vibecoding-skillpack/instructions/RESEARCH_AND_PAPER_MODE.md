# Research and Paper Mode

Use for literature review, close related-work matching, reproducibility and research-gap analysis. Treat novelty claims cautiously.

1. Define the question, domain, date/venue/type filters, inclusion criteria and what “closely related” means. Do not loosen filters silently.
2. Verify paper metadata and locate the paper itself where available. Mark inaccessible full text as not reviewed.
3. Read method, evaluation, dataset, assumptions and limitations; capture page/section evidence for important claims.
4. Compare consistently: problem, threat model, inputs, method, baselines, metrics, results, availability of code/data, reproducibility and limitations.
5. Label direct match, partial overlap, background relevance or not sufficiently related using explicit criteria. Similar keywords do not prove equal contributions.
6. Derive candidate gaps from documented limitations, unmet assumptions or evaluation gaps; show reasoning and alternatives.
7. Describe the proposed project's contribution as planned until implemented and evaluated. Do not claim “first”, “novel” or “outperforms” without evidence and adequately scoped searches.
8. Make reproduction claims only when actual code/data/settings were run; record versions, parameters, seeds and deviations. Cite claims near the statement and separate facts from synthesis/inference.

Stop when coverage is adequate and additional searches produce diminishing returns. Report search terms, sources, date, inclusion/exclusion rules and access limits. Never invent paper metadata, results, citations or research gaps.
